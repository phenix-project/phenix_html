==============================
Mosaic bulk-solvent model
==============================

.. contents::

Overview
--------

The standard (flat) bulk-solvent model fills every part of the unit cell that
is not occupied by the atomic model with the same solvent. The solvent mask
usually consists of one large connected region and a number of isolated
regions: pockets and cavities of the macromolecule. Their content differs
from one to another. Some are empty, some are filled with disordered solvent
partially or fully, and some contain something that is not in the atomic
model yet. Filling all of them like the main solvent puts negative difference
density into the empty ones and is one of the reasons for high R-factors at
low resolution.

The mosaic bulk-solvent model gives each isolated region its own occupancy
relative to the main bulk solvent::

  F_bulk = k_mask * [ F_whole + sum over regions (q_n - 1) * F_n ]

``F_whole`` are the structure factors of the whole mask, as used by the flat
model, ``F_n`` are the structure factors of region n and ``q_n`` is its
occupancy: 0 for an empty region, 1 for a region filled like the main
solvent.

- Regions smaller than ``volume_min`` (50 cubic Angstrom by default, about
  the volume of two water molecules) cannot hold disordered solvent and are
  emptied.
- The occupancies of all other regions are fitted to the data, between 0
  and 1. Only work reflections are used.

The model is used by default in ``phenix.refine`` (final statistics and
maps), ``phenix.maps``, ``phenix.model_vs_data``,
``phenix.model_vs_xray_data``, ``phenix.molprobity``, FEM maps,
``phenix.validate_ligands`` and ``phenix.reciprocal_space_arrays``.
``phenix.mosaic`` is a tool to look at the model for one structure.

Reference: Afonine, P.V., Adams, P.D., Sobolev, O.V. & Urzhumtsev, A.G.
(2024). Accounting for nonuniformity of bulk-solvent: A mosaic model.
*Protein Science* **33**, e4909.

Atoms that are not in the model yet
-----------------------------------

A pocket that contains a ligand that has not been built yet is denser than
bulk solvent. Fitted without a limit, its occupancy comes out above 1, and
the solvent model then takes the ligand density out of the difference map.
The program cannot tell reliably whether a region contains a ligand, so it
does not try. Instead the occupancy is limited to 1: no region gets more
solvent than the flat model gives it. A region that hits the limit is
treated exactly as the flat model treats it, and what it contains in excess
of bulk solvent stays in the difference map. The limit also gives better
R-free at low resolution than a fit without it.

Regions with occupancy 1.00 in the table of regions are either filled like
the main solvent or hold something denser. They are worth a look in the
difference map.

In phenix.refine
----------------

The model is on by default::

  refinement.main.mosaic = True

Refinement itself is done with the flat model. Regions and occupancies are
determined once, at the final bulk-solvent correction and scaling step: the
final R-factors and the output maps are those of the mosaic model. Doing it
at every macro-cycle instead takes about 15 % more time and gives the same
R-free within error (29 test refinements). The regions are listed in the
log (the 20 largest)::

  Mosaic bulk-solvent model (alg4)
    isolated regions of the solvent mask: 82
    smaller than 50 A^3, emptied: 73 (1007 A^3 in total)
    occupancy fitted: 9
    Occupancy is relative to the main bulk solvent: 0 - empty, 1 - same
    (upper limit).
      #  volume(A^3)  occupancy          nearest residues (distance, A)
      1          221       1.00          A GLN84 (2.8), A TYR121 (3.6), ...
      2          136       0.00          A CYS272 (2.8), A LEU157 (2.9), ...
      3          115       0.04          A LEU307 (2.4), A ILE8 (3.1), ...

The volume of a region counts all its symmetry copies in the unit cell. The
residues are those nearest to the centre of the region, with the distance
from the centre to the nearest atom of the residue.

To use the flat model for all of the solvent::

  phenix.refine model.pdb data.mtz main.mosaic=False

Settings are in ``refinement.bulk_solvent_and_scale.mosaic``:

- ``volume_min = 50``: regions smaller than this are emptied. Increase to
  fit fewer regions.
- ``algorithm = alg4``: search for occupancies. ``alg4`` uses the phases of
  the current model and finds all occupancies at once. ``alg2`` uses no
  phases; it is slower and gave about half of the R-factor improvement in
  tests.

The model is not used for twinned data and for neutron data; the log says
so.

phenix.mosaic
-------------

::

  % phenix.mosaic model.pdb data.mtz

Inputs: atomic model (PDB or mmCIF) and X-ray data, with free-R flags if
available. The program scales the data with the flat model and with the
mosaic model and reports R-factors of both (all data and d > 4 A), the table
of regions, and writes ``<prefix>.mtz`` with map coefficients ``2mFo-DFc``,
``mFo-DFc`` (flat model) and ``2mFo-DFc_mosaic``, ``mFo-DFc_mosaic``. The
default prefix is the name of the model file without its extension followed
by ``_mosaic``.

Parameters:

- ``mosaic.volume_min``, ``mosaic.algorithm``: as above;
- ``scattering_table``: ``n_gaussian`` (default), ``wk1995``, ``it1992`` or
  ``electron``;
- ``output.prefix``;
- ``output.write_map_coefficients = True``;
- ``output.write_region_mask = False``: write ``<prefix>_regions.ccp4``, a
  map that shows the fitted regions. The map value in a region is its number
  in the table.

For programmers
---------------

::

  fmodel.update_all_scales(mosaic=True)   # regions and occupancies now
  fmodel.update_all_scales()              # keeps the stored result
  fmodel.remove_mosaic()                  # back to the flat model
  fmodel.mosaic_result()                  # table of regions, or None

``mosaic=True`` is the only switch. Settings are read from ``params.mosaic``
of the bulk-solvent and scaling parameters if ``params`` is given. A call
without ``mosaic=True`` computes nothing new: a stored correction stays part
of F_model, like the mask does, and is dropped when the number of atoms
changes or the mask is optimized. ``fmodel.f_bulk()`` and the ``FMASK``
column written by ``fmodel.export()`` include the mosaic part.
``mmtbx.command_line.load_model_and_data(..., mosaic=True)`` asks for the
model in programs that use this loader (the default is the flat model).
The code is in ``mmtbx/bulk_solvent/mosaic.py``; region structure factors
are computed by direct summation, ``cctbx.maptbx.loft``, or by FFT,
whichever is faster. Symmetry copies of a region are found with
``cctbx.maptbx.connectivity.merge_symmetry_related_regions``.

What to expect
--------------

Numbers below are from 96 structures of the PDB at 0.8 to 4.2 A resolution
(12-13 per resolution range), deposited models and data.

- Without refinement, R-work and R-free are both lower than with the flat
  model by 0.15 percentage points on average. Most of the change is at low
  resolution: for reflections with d > 4 A R-free is lower by 0.46.
- ``phenix.refine`` (29 structures, three macro-cycles): final R-work and
  R-free are both lower by 0.17 on average (R-free better for 24
  structures, worse for 3), with the same R-free/R-work gap.
- At 3.5 A and worse the gain is small and R-free can go either way.
- About 60 % of the fitted regions come out with an occupancy below 0.5.
  The flat model fills them and leaves negative difference density there:
  the number of regions with mean mFo-DFc below -1 sigma goes from 886 to
  147 (of 1840).
- Ligands omitted from the model keep their difference density: for 82
  ligands the mean change is +0.05 sigma, the worst case -0.22 sigma.
- The step takes about three times as long as flat bulk-solvent scaling
  (median 4 s against 1.3 s), mostly because scaling is done twice: with the
  flat model and again with the fitted regions. It is done once in
  ``phenix.refine``, which makes a refinement about 3 % longer. Very large
  unit cells take longer: 150 s against 10 s for a virus capsid with 234
  fitted regions.
- Memory: the search for regions needs the solvent mask of the whole unit
  cell, as the flat model does. Peak memory is the same as with the flat
  model for most structures and up to 1.5 times that for very large unit
  cells (4.8 GB against 3.2 GB for a virus capsid in a 336 million point
  grid).

Data simulated with the flat model (``phenix.fmodel``) contain bulk solvent
in the small regions that the mosaic model empties. Use
``main.mosaic=False`` with such data.

Limitations
-----------

- X-ray (and electron) data only; not for twinned or neutron data.
- At most 300 regions are fitted, the largest ones, and fewer if the
  structure factors of the regions would take more than about 1 GB (very
  many reflections); the smaller regions then stay as in the flat model and
  the log says so. Only virus capsids reached these limits in tests.
- Skipped, with a message in the log, if there are fewer than 20 work
  reflections with bulk-solvent contribution per fitted region, or if bulk
  solvent correction is switched off.
- Occupancies are relative to the scales of the flat model and are
  approximate. An occupancy of 0.1 and one of 0 should not be told apart.
- Negative difference density in a cavity that is already empty (occupancy
  0) is not caused by the solvent model and is not removed by it.
